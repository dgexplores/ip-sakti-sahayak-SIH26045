# International case law: revocation of patents on Indian traditional knowledge

These are the precedents that established documented traditional knowledge as citable prior art abroad, and the direct reason the TKDL exists.

## Turmeric wound-healing patent (USPTO, revoked)
US Patent No. 5,401,504, granted 1995 to the University of Mississippi Medical Center, claimed the use of turmeric for wound healing. The Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) filed for re-examination, submitting documentary evidence, including ancient Sanskrit texts and a published paper, showing the use was long-known in India. The USPTO cancelled the claims, with the re-examination concluding in CSIR's favour in 1997-98. Significantly, the prior art accepted was traditional and non-patent literature, not a prior Western patent.

## Neem antifungal patent (EPO, revoked)
European Patent EP 0436257 B1, granted to W.R. Grace & Co. and the US Department of Agriculture, claimed a method of controlling fungi on plants using hydrophobic extracted neem oil. A challenge brought by Indian civil-society groups and supported in the European Parliament led the EPO to revoke the patent in 2000 for lack of novelty and inventive step, given prior use in India. The revocation was upheld on appeal in 2005.

Implication for Ayurveda:
- Documented traditional knowledge is accepted as prior art by major foreign patent offices. This is the defensive mechanism the TKDL operationalises (see `tkdl_guidelines.md`), by making Indian TK searchable by examiners at the USPTO, EPO and others.
- The same principle cuts both ways for an applicant: if a formulation is substantially the classical, documented preparation, expect it to be refused or revoked abroad on TK prior art, exactly as it would face the Sec 3(p) bar in India.
- These cases are the historical driver behind the WIPO GRATK Treaty 2024 disclosure requirement (see `wipo_gratk_2024.md`), which shifts the burden onto applicants to disclose the origin of genetic resources and associated TK up front, rather than leaving revocation as the only remedy after the fact.
