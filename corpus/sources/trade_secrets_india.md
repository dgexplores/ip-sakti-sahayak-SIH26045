# Trade Secrets in India: contract and common law (no dedicated statute)

India has no standalone trade secrets Act. Protection is built from three sources, and this is the key practical point for an Ayurveda business.
- Indian Contract Act, 1872, Sec 27: an agreement restraining a person from exercising a lawful trade or profession is void. Confidentiality obligations that operate *during* employment or protect genuinely secret information are generally enforceable. Broad post-employment non-compete clauses usually are not.
- Common law breach of confidence: an action lies where information was confidential, was shared in circumstances importing an obligation of confidence, and was used without authorisation.
- Contractual instruments: NDAs, employment confidentiality clauses, supplier and contract-manufacturer agreements.

Implication for Ayurveda:
- A proprietary formulation kept genuinely secret can be protected indefinitely as a trade secret, with no filing, no fee and no expiry. This is the main alternative when a formulation cannot clear the Sec 3(p) traditional-knowledge bar under the Patents Act.
- The trade-off against a patent: a trade secret gives no protection against a competitor who independently develops or reverse-engineers the same formulation, and it is lost permanently once disclosed.
- A trade secret cannot be used to withhold what regulation requires disclosed. Ingredient and label disclosure under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act or FSSAI rules, and origin/source disclosure of biological resources under the Biological Diversity Act and WIPO GRATK, still apply. Secrecy is not a defence to an ABS obligation.
- Practical steps: written NDAs with staff and contract manufacturers, restricted access to the batch-manufacturing record, and documented internal handling, so the "circumstances importing an obligation of confidence" can actually be evidenced later.
