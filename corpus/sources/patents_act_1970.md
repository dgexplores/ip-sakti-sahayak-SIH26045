# Patents Act, 1970 — Key provisions for Ayurveda

## Sec 3(p) — Not patentable (TK bar)
An invention which, in effect, is traditional knowledge or an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component(s) — is not an invention within the meaning of this Act.

> Exact span: "an invention which in effect, is traditional knowledge or an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component or components." — Sec 3(p), Patents Act, 1970.
Source: https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/portal/ev/sections/ps3.html

Implication for Ayurveda:
- Classical formulation verbatim from Charaka/Samhita → Sec 3(p) bar, no patent. Defend via TKDL prior art.
- Proprietary with novel ratio/process/dosage → patentable if it passes novelty + inventive step (Sec 2(1)(j), 2(1)(ja)).

## Sec 3(d) — mere discovery
Mere discovery of a new form/property of known substance without enhanced efficacy — not patentable.

## Sec 10 — Complete specification
Requires best method, deposit of biological material under Budapest Treaty if not otherwise available.

## Sec 25 — Opposition (incl. TK ground)
TKDL-backed opposition under Sec 25(1)(k) and 25(2)(k).
