# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary users are people in India working with Ayurveda knowledge and products: practitioners, students, small product founders, and community innovators. SIH judges and mentors are a secondary audience evaluating whether the assistant is clear, grounded, and safe.

## Product Purpose

IP-SAKTI Sahayak helps a person understand what Indian intellectual-property and regulatory rules may apply to an Ayurveda-related question. Its immediate job is to turn a plain-language question into an understandable answer, a practical next step, and official-source evidence.

## Positioning

The product keeps Indian and international legal regimes separate and grounds every answer in quoted source material rather than presenting unsupported legal advice.

## Operating Context

People may arrive with incomplete legal vocabulary, use Hindi, Tamil, or English, and access the tool on a phone. They need to decide whether to proceed, research further, or seek expert help—not read a legal database.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- India and international legal answers are deliberately separated by a jurisdiction control.
- The product supports English, Hindi, and Tamil UI text.
- Responses provide confidence, quotations, source links, and an escalation path.
- The corpus is a focused set of legal summaries; the product must clearly distinguish guidance from legal advice.
- The redesign must preserve the citation-grounded and safety-first answer model.

## Brand Commitments

The existing name, IP-SAKTI Sahayak, remains. The interface should feel trustworthy, welcoming, direct, and recognisably Indian without relying on stereotypes or over-decoration.

## Evidence on Hand

The repository contains a working FastAPI/Next.js application, a 23-document legal corpus, source citations, multilingual UI copy, and automated backend/frontend checks. There are no verified user testimonials or external performance claims to display.

## Product Principles

1. Start with the person’s decision, not legal terminology.
2. State the direct answer before presenting legal detail.
3. Make Indian-law guidance the natural default while preserving a visible international path.
4. Let people act from the answer: understand, verify, or escalate.
5. Be calm and transparent when the system is uncertain.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use clear language, keyboard-accessible controls, sufficient contrast, large mobile touch targets, reduced-motion support, and meaningful screen-reader labels.
